# Last Dividend: Lore Bible

*Everything here is fiction. No real politicians, parties, companies or brands. The satire is aimed at the system, not at a side: whichever way a debate goes, the executive gets paid.*

## The premise

You are one of six executives in **Port Calder**, a harbour city that stands in for every Western city of the last fifty years. A run climbs society from the bottom up across three acts, and each act is an era:

| Act | Name | Year | Where you are | What the city argues about |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Street** | 1978 | A walk-up office over the docks | Mills closing, unions shrinking, rents rising, broken-windows policing, selling off the water works |
| II | **The Middle** | 2008 | A glass tower downtown | The crash and the bailout, lobbying, clickbait news, regulators who used to work for you, gentrification |
| III | **The Top** | Now | A resilience estate on your own island | Climate collapse, billionaire bunkers, tech monopolies, inequality, populism from both ends, trust breaking, then **The Reckoning** |

The bunker grows with you: a **1978 fallout shelter** (civil-defence surplus, canned goods, diesel), a **2008 prepper vault** (servers, a wine wall, a panic room), then a **techno-luxury resilience estate** (hydroponics, a house AI, a sky garden).

## The rule of the satire

Every debate has two loud sides. Your executive sells to both.
- Rent control or property rights? You own the buildings either way.
- More police or fewer? You bill the overtime and sell the private security.
- Regulate or deregulate? You write the regulation, then hire the regulator.
- Hearth rally or Forward march? You sponsor both stages.

**Political furniture (fictional):** the **Hearth Party** (tradition, order, "common sense") and the **Forward League** (progress, experts, "the right side of history"). The city paper is **The Ledger** (later *Ledger.now*). The 2008 rescue is the **Stability Fund**; the bank that failed is **Calder Mutual**; the dark-money group is **Citizens for Prosperity**. Phones are fictional: the rotary desk set, **the Berry** pager-phone, the smart ring.

## Timeline of Port Calder

- **1971** Calder Steel banks its first furnace.
- **1975** The state refuses a bailout: *STATE TO CALDER: SINK OR SWIM.*
- **1977** The blackout. Looting on Harbor Ave. A crackdown follows.
- **1978 — Act I.** Gas lines, rent strikes on Odell Street, Local 9 walks out, the 14th Precinct starts "quality of life" sweeps. The mayor floats selling the water works. You buy the waterfront for pennies.
- **1981** The Clean Streets Act. **1986** The Public Assets Disposal Act: the water works is sold. Guess to whom.
- **1990s** Both parties discover they love markets. Welfare is "reformed". The Ledger gets a website.
- **2008 — Act II.** Calder Mutual collapses. The Stability Fund passes. Foreclosures in the Flats; *The Brownstone District* on Odell Street. Citizens for Prosperity buys every ad slot. The regulator joins the firm she regulated.
- **2010s** Square occupations from the Forward side, Hearth revival rallies from the other. Both blame the other.
- **Now — Act III.** Heat domes, a failing sea wall, insurers leaving the coast, one app owning the buses and the bank. Trust in institutions at a record low. **The Reckoning** (superstorm, heat dome or sea wall) closes the fiscal year.

## Endings

- **Paid Off**: you won while playing any cash card or Cost of Doing Business, or you Settled. They take the envelope (6th "paid" pose where drawn).
- **Crushed**: you won on pressure alone. Defeated pose.
- **They Walked**: they got through. They leave with what they came for.
Each ending is stored in the run (`fy.fates[id] = { how, q, act }`) and shows **"They'll remember this."** for Acts I–II.

## Tone guide

- Short, dry, specific. Comic bubbles, not speeches.
- Every character believes they are the reasonable one.
- Name the system, not a side. If a joke only lands on one party, add the mirror joke or cut it.
- No real names, brands, logos or slogans. Invent: the Berry, Ledger.now, Calder Mutual, Citizens for Prosperity.
